## The challenge

When I began working with [Schneitter Fireworks](https://www.schneitterfireworks.com/) in 2016, the company had decades of industry knowledge, long-standing supplier relationships, and a respected place in the fireworks business. What it did not yet have was a complete, modern brand system or a clearly defined market position that matched everything it was capable of becoming.

Schneitter had reached a crossroads: keep operating inside an increasingly limiting model, or rethink how it presented itself, served customers, negotiated with suppliers, and pursued growth.

What started as hands-on creative support grew into a long-term leadership role. I helped shape not only how Schneitter looked, but how it thought about growth, customer service, supplier relationships, product access, and its place in the wholesale fireworks market.

## My role

Contract Creative Director across brand strategy, business positioning, supplier-strategy input, catalog-system development, website direction, product photography, direct-mail strategy, digital marketing, advertising, Danger Doll private-label development, packaging direction, sales messaging, creative training, team development, and final brand approval.

## The approach

One of the most important shifts was helping position Schneitter as a true one-stop shop for independent fireworks retailers.

### Make wholesale more flexible

Through relationships with Chinese manufacturers, Schneitter could offer direct-ship containers to larger customers seeking full-container volume. At the same time, I helped champion mixed containers assembled from the company’s centrally located Missouri warehouse.

That gave independent retailers a more flexible path. Instead of committing to a full container from a single brand or manufacturer, customers could mix and match across a broader selection. Retailers got more variety, more control over inventory, and products that better matched their local markets.

That shift became more than an operational advantage. It became a central part of the company’s value proposition and customer messaging.

### Negotiate from a stronger position

I was also instrumental in helping the company rethink supplier relationships.

At the time, Schneitter was too often accepting unfavorable treatment and operating with limited leverage. I pushed for a more confident approach: direct negotiation, face-to-face conversations, clearer expectations, and a willingness to walk away from suppliers that did not value the relationship.

Rather than feeling locked into existing arrangements, the company began approaching manufacturers and brand partners with greater confidence. We became more willing to evaluate alternatives, negotiate from a stronger position, and move business toward suppliers that offered better products, service, terms, and long-term partnership.

That strengthened Schneitter’s ability to protect its margins, expand its product mix, and make decisions based on what was best for the company and its customers.

### Build systems that scale beyond one designer

Creative work translated that broader strategy into a stronger wholesale brand through annual catalogs, direct-mail campaigns, price lists, digital advertising, social media, website development, product photography, sales materials, and customer communications.

The annual wholesale catalog became one of the most important expressions of the brand. I designed the catalog system from the ground up: structure, visual hierarchy, page architecture, product presentation, and overall creative direction. Each new catalog is built from that foundation and refreshed annually for new products, priorities, and seasonal goals.

I followed the same approach across advertising, mail pieces, price books, and digital assets, creating repeatable design systems that could evolve without losing brand consistency.

### Launch what Schneitter could own

Another major initiative was Danger Doll, Schneitter Fireworks’ private-label fireworks brand. I developed the original brand identity, visual direction, logo, packaging concepts, and supporting marketing materials. Danger Doll gave Schneitter a distinctive product line it could own, promote, and grow independently within the wholesale market.

### Grow from creator to brand steward

As Schneitter expanded, my role evolved with it.

In the early years, I personally handled nearly every aspect of the creative process, from concept through production. As marketing needs grew, I transitioned from hands-on creator to trainer, teaching others how to use the systems, standards, and creative processes I had established.

Today I serve primarily as creative leader and final brand steward. I review, direct, refine, and approve work from the internal team so catalogs, advertisements, digital materials, and customer communications stay consistent with the brand foundation I originally built.

That evolution is one of the achievements I am most proud of. The goal was never to make the company dependent on me for every design. The goal was to build a creative system, develop the people around it, and create a brand strong enough to keep growing.

## The impact

Over the course of the partnership, Schneitter Fireworks evolved from a business facing an uncertain future into a thriving national importer and wholesale partner. Annual container volume grew to approximately three times its earlier level, supported by stronger branding, expanded product access, clearer customer communication, more confident supplier negotiations, and a more flexible wholesale model.

The work left behind a modernized wholesale brand, a stronger one-stop-shop position for independent retailers, improved leverage with suppliers, greater customer access through direct-ship and mixed-container programs, the launch of Danger Doll, and a scalable creative system that still guides catalogs, advertising, digital presence, and long-term growth.
